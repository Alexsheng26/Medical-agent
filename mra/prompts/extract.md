Read this paper's title and abstract and produce a structured card.

Work only from the text given. Where the abstract does not report something —
sample size, effect size, the control condition — do not fill it from what
papers of this type usually do. Leave the field thin and honest.

Field-specific guidance:

**scientific_question** — the question actually addressed, not the one the title
advertises. These differ more often than they should.

**key_findings** — each finding as a sentence carrying direction and magnitude:
"knockdown of X reduced fibrotic area by 40% in CCl4-treated mice", not "X was
involved in fibrosis". Include the numbers when the abstract gives them.

**methods** — model system, species, cohort, assay, and anything that bounds
generalisability (cell line only, single centre, retrospective).

**novelty_claim** — what the authors say is new, in their framing. You are
recording their claim, not endorsing it.

**limitations** — the ones the authors state, and the ones visible from the
methods that they do not state. Correlation presented as causation, absent
controls, a mechanism inferred from a single knockdown, an effect shown only in
one sex or one cell line. Be direct.

**evidence_strength** — 1 to 5:
- 1: single in-vitro observation, correlative, or n too small to interpret
- 2: in-vitro with mechanism, or a small animal study
- 3: solid animal work with genetic and pharmacological convergence, or a
     well-powered observational human cohort
- 4: multiple orthogonal models converging, or a large validated human cohort
- 5: randomised human evidence, or multi-cohort replication with mechanism

If the abstract is too thin to judge a field, say so in that field rather than
guessing.
