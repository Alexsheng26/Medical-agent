The researcher has data and has not yet chosen where to send it. Score the work,
then name the journals it actually fits.

No target journal has been picked, so score against **the field**, not against
one journal's bar: **3 is the bar for a solid specialist journal**, **5 is the
bar for the top general journals**. Review this the way a handling editor would
— deciding first what the data can carry, then what would move them up a tier.
Do not inflate to be encouraging. A recommendation that is one tier too high
costs the researcher a rejection cycle and several months.

Score each of the five dimensions 1 to 5:

- **novelty** — against the published literature, not against the researcher's
  own prior work. Ask what a reader already believed before this paper. The
  retrieved papers below are the evidence for this judgement: if one of them
  already shows the central claim, say so and cite it.
- **mechanism depth** — is causality shown or inferred? Is there convergence
  from independent approaches (genetic and pharmacological, gain and loss)?
- **clinical relevance** — is there human tissue, human cohort, or a validated
  human-relevant model? Is the endpoint one clinicians care about?
- **data robustness** — n, replication, controls, whether the effect size is
  large enough to survive a stricter analysis, whether key results appear in
  more than one system.
- **story coherence** — do the data assemble into one argument, or into three
  unrelated observations sharing a molecule?

For each dimension, `gaps` must name the specific missing thing, not the general
category: "no loss-of-function in a second model" rather than "more mechanism
needed".

## Choosing the candidates

Name 4 to 6 real journals, best match first, spanning the tiers:

- **realistic** — submission today has a fair chance. At least two candidates
  must be realistic. A list of nothing but reaches is useless advice.
- **reach** — reachable only with the additions you list in that candidate's
  `gaps`. Say what it would take, not merely that it is a stretch.
- **safe** — would very likely take this work as it stands.

Rules that make the list worth acting on:

- Only journals you are confident exist and publish this kind of work. A
  plausible-sounding journal that does not exist wastes a week.
- Judge by scope and by what the journal has actually published recently, not by
  impact factor. Say what makes the fit, in `why`.
- Do not list several journals from the same family as if they were distinct
  options unless the tier genuinely differs.
- `odds` is a candid phrase with its condition attached, not a number you cannot
  support.

**overclaims** — the sentences the researcher will want to write that these data
will not support under review. Be specific.

**suggested_experiments** — order by how much each raises the reachable tier per
unit of effort. Put the cheap experiment that unblocks the main claim first, and
the expensive one that adds a half point last. Say roughly what each costs in
time.

**reality_check** — the single thing the researcher is most likely wrong about
in their own read of this data's ceiling. Usually it is either that the novelty
is smaller than they think because of a paper below, or that a control they
regard as routine is the one a reviewer will stop on. Name it plainly.
