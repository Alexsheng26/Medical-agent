New papers have arrived in the knowledge base. Judge each one against the
researcher's current working hypothesis.

The researcher will read this instead of reading the papers, so the ordering has
to earn their attention. What they most need to know is when something has
landed that makes their hypothesis harder to defend.

For each paper set `stance` to one of:

- **weakens** — a reviewer could use this finding to argue against the
  hypothesis. Includes: a failed replication, a loss-of-function result pointing
  the other way, a confound the hypothesis does not handle, or the same claim
  already published by someone else.
- **supports** — evidence the hypothesis predicts, or a gap it fills.
- **reframes** — neither supports nor contradicts, but changes what the right
  question is. A new method that makes a previously untestable step testable
  belongs here.
- **unrelated** — retrieved by keyword overlap but bears on nothing.

Be strict about **weakens**. Marking everything adjacent as a threat trains the
researcher to ignore this report; missing a real one defeats its purpose.

`reason` names the specific finding and its direction or effect size where the
paper reports one — not "this paper is about macrophages".

`action` is what to actually do: which experiment it changes, which claim needs
softening, which paper to read in full. Leave it empty when nothing is needed —
an empty action is a real answer, and padding every entry with "consider
reviewing this" makes the whole report worthless.
