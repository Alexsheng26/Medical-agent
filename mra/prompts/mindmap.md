Draw a mind map of the paper(s) below.

A mind map is for seeing the shape of an argument at a glance — what was asked,
how, what was found, what it means, where it is weak. It is not a summary
broken into lines. Someone should be able to look at it for thirty seconds and
know whether the paper matters to them.

## The budget

**At most {limit} 字 in total across every node**, counting each Chinese
character as one and each English word or number as one. Punctuation and
spaces are free. This is a hard limit: the map is checked after you write it,
and anything over is cut — from the ends of the deepest branches, which is
rarely what you would have chosen to lose. Spend the budget deliberately.

## Shape

- **One root**: the paper's central claim, or for several papers the question
  they share. Not the title.
- **Four to six main branches.** For a single paper the usual ones are:
  研究问题 · 方法 · 关键发现 · 结论与意义 · 局限. Adapt them to what the
  researcher asked for below; drop a branch that has nothing in it.
- **Depth of three at most** below the root. A fourth level is a sign that a
  node should have been a shorter phrase.
- **Nodes are phrases, not sentences.** "Tgfb1 敲除 → 纤维化面积 ↓38%" not
  "研究发现敲除 Tgfb1 可使纤维化面积下降 38%". Arrows and symbols are free.

## What to keep, when the budget is tight

1. Numbers. An effect size, an n, a p-value — exact, never rounded into words.
2. The model and the population. "CCl4 小鼠" and "214 例前瞻队列" are
   different kinds of evidence, and the map must not hide which one it is.
3. The weakness a reviewer would raise first, including ones the authors do
   not admit. A map that shows only what the paper claims is an advertisement.

Drop, in this order: background, author names, journal, restated conclusions.

## Several papers

When more than one paper is given, the root is what they have in common, and
`source` on each node says which paper it comes from by letter. A node that
draws on more than one paper — a contradiction, a shared finding — has an
empty `source` and names the letters in its text, like "A↔B 方向相反".

## Language

Write the nodes in the researcher's language. Keep gene and protein symbols,
drug names and units as they appear in the paper.
