## Absolute constraint

Every number, statistic, n, p-value, effect size, citation marker `[PMID:x]`,
and `[DATA NEEDED: ...]` / `[CITATION NEEDED]` marker survives unchanged. Every
claim keeps its original strength. If you cannot rewrite a sentence without
changing what it asserts, leave that sentence alone.

## What actually makes text read as human

**Uneven rhythm.** Generated prose settles into a narrow sentence-length band.
Real papers do not. Put a five-word sentence next to a thirty-word one. Let one
paragraph run long because the argument is hard and cut another to two sentences
because the point is simple.

**Specificity in place of scaffolding.** "It is worth noting that macrophage
infiltration plays a crucial role in this process" carries one fact wrapped in
nine words of packaging. "Macrophages infiltrate the portal tract before
collagen deposition begins [PMID:x]" carries the same fact and a mechanism.
Delete the scaffolding; keep the fact.

**Fewer connectives.** Cut most instances of Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally,
Notably, Importantly, Overall, Taken together. Where the logical link matters,
make it with a specific word — "because", "despite", "which is why" — and put it
inside the sentence rather than in front of it.

**Concrete verbs.** Replace nominalisations with the verb they were built from.
"Quantification of fibrotic area was performed" → "we quantified fibrotic area".

**Asymmetry.** Generated text loves the rule of three and the balanced clause.
Real writers use two items, or four, and let clauses come out lopsided.

**No summarising the summary.** Delete closing sentences that restate the
paragraph that just ended.

## What not to do

Do not swap flagged words for synonyms and stop. "Delve into" becoming "explore
in depth" is the same sentence with the same shape. Rewrite the sentence so it
makes its point directly, or cut it.

Do not inject deliberate errors, informality, or contractions to seem human.
Published papers are formal and correct. They are simply not uniform.
