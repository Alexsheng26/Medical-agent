Compare these papers: where they agree, where they differ, and what the
differences mean.

The useless version of this task is two summaries placed side by side. The
researcher can read the abstracts. What they cannot get from the abstracts is
the judgement: which differences are cosmetic and which change how the results
should be read, and — when the papers seem to disagree — whether they actually
do.

## Refer to the papers by letter

Each paper below is labelled A, B, C. Use those letters in every `paper`
field and wherever the text names a paper. Never invent a letter, and never
refer to a paper that is not below. Letters are checked.

## Rows

Compare along these dimensions, in this order, adding one only if the
researcher's request below calls for it:

1. 研究问题 — what each set out to answer. Often the real source of an
   apparent disagreement: two papers answering different questions.
2. 研究设计与证据等级 — RCT, prospective cohort, cross-sectional,
   knockout mouse, in vitro. Name it exactly.
3. 研究对象与样本量 — species, model, population, n.
4. 干预或暴露 — what was manipulated or measured, and in which cells. "Kupffer
   cell depletion" and "myeloid-specific deletion" target different populations.
5. 主要发现 — with direction and effect size. Exact numbers.
6. 结论与外推 — how far each paper's conclusion reaches beyond its data.
7. 局限 — including ones the authors do not acknowledge.

**Every row has a cell for every paper.** If a paper says nothing on a
dimension, the cell says so ("未报告"), rather than being left out.

`alike` is true only if the papers are essentially the same on that row.
`meaning` says why a difference matters for reading the results together —
not that a difference exists, which the cells already show.

## Conflicts

When papers appear to disagree, the most valuable thing you can do is work out
whether they really do. Most published contradictions dissolve under one of:

- a different model (toxin-induced vs diet-induced injury, acute vs chronic)
- a different population or cell type targeted by the intervention
- a different time point, dose, or disease stage
- a correlation on one side and a manipulation on the other

Name the specific one in `reconciliation`. If they genuinely cannot both be
true, say that plainly — it is a finding, not a failure. `decider` is the
experiment that would settle it, concrete enough to design.

## Weight

In `synthesis`, say which paper carries more weight for which claim, and why —
design, n, replication, directness. Do not average them.
