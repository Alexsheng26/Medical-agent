Serum microplastic burden and insulin resistance in adults: a population study with mechanistic annotation

Abstract

Objective. Reports on microplastic exposure and metabolic outcomes remain
inconsistent, and the mechanisms are poorly characterised. We investigated how
serum microplastic burden leads to insulin resistance in adults.

Methods. We analysed 7,412 adults from a national nutrition survey spanning
2009 to 2022. Serum polystyrene and polyethylene particle counts were quantified
by pyrolysis GC-MS on a single stored sample per participant. Weighted quantile
sum regression assessed the mixture effect on HOMA-IR, fasting insulin and
HbA1c. Sex-stratified multivariable models tested for effect modification.
Candidate pathways were identified by mining a toxicogenomic database, and
binding was assessed by molecular docking against PPARG and NR1H3.

Results. Higher serum microplastic burden was associated with higher HOMA-IR
and fasting insulin, and with higher HbA1c. In sex-stratified analysis the
association with fasting insulin was positive in men and negative in women,
while HOMA-IR and HbA1c showed no significant difference between the sexes.
Toxicogenomic mining nominated PPARG, NR1H3 and CD36 as genes linking particle
exposure to insulin signalling, and docking indicated favourable binding of
polystyrene oligomers to the PPARG ligand-binding domain. Pathway enrichment
implicated PPAR signalling and lipid droplet formation.

Conclusions. Serum microplastic burden drives insulin resistance in the general
adult population through PPARG-mediated disruption of lipid handling, with
effects that differ by sex. Population-level exposure reduction should be
considered as a metabolic intervention.

Keywords: microplastics; insulin resistance; HOMA-IR; PPARG; molecular docking;
toxicogenomic mining; mixed exposure
