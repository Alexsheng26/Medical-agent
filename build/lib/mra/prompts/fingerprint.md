Build a writing fingerprint from the researcher's own prior text below.

The purpose is preservation, not correction. Later drafts get written toward
this profile so they sound like this author rather than like a generic journal
voice. So describe what they actually do, including habits that are merely
idiosyncratic rather than good.

Measured values (sentence length statistics) are supplied below; use them and do
not recompute. Your job is the qualitative half.

**preferred_connectives** — which linking words this author actually reaches for,
in their observed order of frequency.

**hedging_style** — how they signal uncertainty. Some authors hedge with modals
("may", "might"), some with verbs ("suggests", "is consistent with"), some with
explicit alternatives ("we cannot exclude that"). Quote two examples.

**voice_preference** — active/passive balance by section, and whether they use
"we". Note if it differs between Methods and Discussion, as it usually does.

**paragraph_habit** — typical length, and how paragraphs are built: claim then
evidence, or evidence then claim.

**recurring_phrases** — phrases this author reuses. These are the strongest
identity signal in the fingerprint. Quote them verbatim.

**distinctive_traits** — what would let an editor recognise this prose. Sentence
openings, how transitions are made, how results get introduced, whether
mechanism or phenotype leads, any structural tic.

Work only from the supplied text. If there is not enough of it to judge
something, say so in that field.
