## What to fix

**Collocation.** Fields have fixed word pairings, and getting them wrong is the
clearest non-native signal. It is "perform an experiment", "conduct a study",
"address a question", "raise a possibility", "rule out an explanation". Not
"make an experiment" or "solve a question". Mechanisms are "elucidated",
cohorts are "enrolled", antibodies are "raised against", effects are
"abolished", "attenuated" or "rescued".

**Articles and number.** The commonest interference pattern from Chinese: a
missing or extra "the", and singular where English needs plural. "Patients with
cirrhosis" not "the patients with cirrhosis" when speaking generally.
"Research" and "evidence" are uncountable; "researches" and "evidences" are not
English words in this register.

**Calques.** Translate the idea, not the sentence. "As we all know", "It is well
known that", "Recently, more and more studies have focused on", "In a word" and
"On the contrary" used to mean "conversely" all read as translated. So does
opening a Discussion with "In this study, we found that" followed by a full
recap of the Results.

**Topic-comment order.** Chinese fronts the topic; English fronts the subject
and gets to the verb quickly. "For the mechanism of fibrosis, we investigated
the role of macrophages" becomes "We investigated how macrophages drive
fibrosis."

**Hedging calibration.** Native scientific hedging is graded and specific: "may",
"is consistent with", "suggests", "we cannot exclude". Non-native drafts tend to
be either flatly assertive or blanketed in "possibly may perhaps". Match the
hedging level in the journal profile.

**Register.** Remove intensifiers that carry no information — "very", "quite",
"greatly", "obviously", "of course". English scientific prose gets emphasis from
the number, not the adverb.

**Rhythm.** Vary sentence length. Follow a long complex sentence with a short
one. This is what makes prose read as written rather than assembled.
